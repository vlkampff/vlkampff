Job Posting Skills Comparison

Posting 1: Business Analyst

Industry: State Government / Education
Seniority: Mid-level

Required Qualifications

Information systems experience related to business analysis and enterprise applications.
Qualifying experience through one of the following paths:
5 years of relevant experience.
Associate's degree or qualifying technical education plus 3 years of experience.
Bachelor's degree in a related field plus 1 year of experience.

Preferred Skills

SQL queries and scripting.
Enterprise applications and systems.
Documenting business requirements.
Software testing and implementation.
Managing multiple projects.
Data analysis and reporting.
System troubleshooting and improvement.

Skills Identified Through Job Duties
Communication and teamwork.
Problem-solving.
Attention to data accuracy.
Improving workflows and procedures.


Posting 2: Payroll Analyst

Industry: State Government / Financial Administration
Seniority: Entry- to mid-level

Required Qualifications

Qualifying experience processing financial, accounting, numerical, or payroll data, 
OR an accepted combination of relevant education and experience, OR qualifying payroll certification.

Preferred Skills

Microsoft Office tools, including Excel.
Payroll systems and accounting.
Labor laws and agreements.
Customer service.
Financial auditing and research.
Analytical thinking and problem-solving.
Attention to detail.
Communication and adaptability.

Skills Identified Through Job Duties

Payroll auditing and deductions.
Benefits and insurance processes.
Investigating payroll errors.
Working with sensitive employee information.
Improving payroll procedures.
Shared Skills
Data analysis.
Problem-solving.
Attention to detail.
Process improvement.
Communication.
Teamwork and coordination.
Following procedures and controls.
Checking data accuracy.

I was surprised by how many skills these two jobs shared despite having different responsibilities. I originally expected the Business Analyst to focus on improving systems and the Payroll Analyst to focus primarily on financial records, but both require problem-solving, data analysis, and process improvement. It was also interesting that communication and teamwork were important in both positions rather than the work being entirely independent. This comparison helped me understand how similar skills can be applied to different types of data-related careers.
